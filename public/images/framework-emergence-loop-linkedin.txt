I rebuilt my AI agent framework for the fifth time and figured something was wrong with me.

It wasn't. The version that finally worked didn't appear because I'd gotten smarter about the first design. Every rebuild encoded a lesson the previous version couldn't survive, and the working version was just what that accumulation produced.

The trap is premature convergence, the urge to lock a design down before it meets real conditions. You ship a clean v1 that solves the problems you knew about, then production shows those were never the real ones. Six months of bolt-ons later, you admit a v2 has to exist.

I used to read the rebuilds as proof the last version failed. They were the mechanism that made the next one better. I'd taken the loop for a symptom of doing it wrong, but it's the process itself.

Where in your own work are you calling the rebuild a failure when it's just how the design gets found?
