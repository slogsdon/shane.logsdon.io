key findings

The gap is not brand authority — it is data layer and content architecture.

01 · finding

Zero structured data sitewide means AI agents cannot determine what Mercer does, who it serves, or what services it offers from machine-readable signals alone.

02 · finding

Absent from 6 of 7 non-branded queries: a firm ranked #1 by Barron's is invisible to buyers who do not already know the name.

03 · finding

The "family office for every family" positioning maps to an unoccupied search space — no competitor owns the $2M–$15M mid-market family office query, and Mercer is not in it.

04 · finding

NAPFA directories hold positions 1, 5, and 7 on fee-only fiduciary queries; a free NAPFA listing would resolve this without a single content investment.

05 · finding

llms.txt is genuinely best-in-class — comprehensive, current, 110+ FAQ entries indexed — but AI citation engines also weight structured data and FAQ schema, both of which are absent.

recommended next steps

Five moves unlock the visibility that brand authority alone cannot.

01 · step

Add Organization and Service JSON-LD schema sitewide — one sprint that resolves the entire data-layer gap and immediately improves AI citation accuracy.

02 · step

Submit to NAPFA's Find-an-Advisor directory — free, one submission, and it places Mercer inside a SERP where NAPFA currently holds three of ten positions.

03 · step

Rewrite the homepage opening as a Quick Answer block: replace the award-first lede with a two-sentence definition of what Mercer does, for whom, and what makes it different.

04 · step

Publish an integrated wealth management service page combining tax, estate, investment, and planning explicitly — this addresses the highest-traffic competitive query where the firm is currently absent.

05 · step

Build a long-form piece targeting the $2M–$15M family office buyer — the only unoccupied SERP position in this audit; Mercer is uniquely positioned to own it.
