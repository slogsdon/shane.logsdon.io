Round 2 — five color worlds on one architecture

The experiment changed. Round 1 varied the architecture and held color constant; you picked B on livability, not on its structural trick. So B's architecture is now the constant across all five — reading-first, rail + hairline spine + 60ch measure — and the two variables are a color world and a visual layer. The type spine (Fraunces + IBM Plex + JetBrains Mono) is unchanged from v2, because that's the thread that keeps this recognizably yours.

Every figure is drawn from the real articles-list.json — 32 posts, 23 of them between 2013 and 2017, a nine-year gap, then 9 in 2026. Nothing here is invented data and nothing is a texture applied for atmosphere; each visual layer is a figure that carries information and a caption that says where it came from.

Direction 1

Riso

The system is printed with two spot inks on newsprint stock, and it doesn't hide it. Olive and a hot coral each get their own pass; where they overlap they multiply into a third color you never specify directly. The headline carries a 2px misregistration because a two-pass press produces one.

Where the visual layer comes from: halftone screens. Density of dots encodes the data, so the texture is the figure rather than a background applied behind it.

Palette
Newsprint #f2efe4 replaces cream. Olive stays. Coral #e04e2f is the new second ink; overprint #3f3a1e is a result, not a token you pick.
Visual layer
Halftone dot screens at varying density, drawn as SVG patterns. Deliberate misregistration on display type only — never on body copy, where it would hurt legibility.
Why it's livable
Two inks is a rule, not a mood. Any new page type inherits it automatically: one ink for structure, one for signal, overprint where they meet.
Risk
Riso is having a moment in indie print and zine design — it's distinct on the web but not un-trendy in general. The misregistration reads as a bug to anyone who doesn't know the reference.

Direction 2

Drafting

The visual layer is technical drawing convention used honestly: dimension lines with witness marks, hatch fill for a region under discussion, and numbered callout bubbles in the margin keyed to points in the text. Diagrams stop being illustrations of the argument and become part of its apparatus.

Color gains one member with a real job: annotation red. It only ever marks something the drawing is calling out — a dimension, a callout number, a correction. It never styles a heading.

Palette
Cream and ink survive untouched from v2 — this is the smallest color change of the five. Olive keeps links and states. Annotation red #c0392b is added with a strict single role.
Visual layer
Dimension lines, witness marks, 45° hatch, callout bubbles. Drawn in SVG at 1px to match the system's existing rule weight — the diagram and the page share a line.
Why it's livable
Closest to v2 of anything here, so nothing already built breaks. The callout apparatus is a genuine writing tool you'd use in long-form posts, not a home-page ornament.
Risk
It only pays off if you actually draw diagrams. A drafting system with no drawings in it is just cream with a red accent, and that's a weaker place than v2.

Direction 3

Plotted

The visual layer is your own data, plotted. Publishing cadence, category weight, the nine-year gap — the site charts itself, and the charts are the decoration because there isn't any other. This is the direction that matches how you actually judge things: evidence over demos, a claim you can click through to.

Color widens by turning the single olive into a five-step sequential ramp, so the palette gains range without gaining hues. Amber is the one signal color and it marks exactly one thing per chart.

Palette
Olive becomes a ramp #e7ebdd → #3e5020. Amber #b06f16 is the signal. Paper cools slightly to #f7f7f4 so the ramp's light end still separates from it.
Visual layer
Real charts from articles-list.json, drawn in the system's own line weights. Every figure caption names its source and the date it was read.
Why it's livable
The ramp is a genuine system asset — it covers every chart, table heat, and category weight you'll ever need, which the single olive could not.
Risk
Needs data to stay honest. A chart of nothing is worse than no chart, and the gap only reads as candor once — after that it's a bit that needs retiring.

Direction 4

Survey

The largest color departure in the set: sand stock and sepia ink instead of cream and near-black. The visual layer is topographic — contour lines whose elevation is real (how deeply a subject has been worked), with a legend and grid references that double as the site's navigation.

This is the one that stops looking like the current site. It's also the one where the metaphor has to justify itself on every page, not just the home page.

Palette
Sand #f0e9dc replaces cream; sepia #33291f replaces near-black. Olive warms to #6b7f45 to sit on sand; signal orange #cc5b25 marks the current position.
Visual layer
Contour rings drawn from category depth, a legend block, and grid references. The legend is a real nav component — it appears on every page type.
Why it's livable
A legend and a key scale to any content. And warm sepia is genuinely easier on the eye for long reading than #0e1116 on white.
Risk
Highest of the five. Cartography-as-metaphor gets twee fast, and sand-and-sepia is one step from a coffee-shop menu. It works only if the contours stay tied to data.

Direction 5

Ledger Stock

The material is accounting paper: pale green banded stock, a red thread rule at the top and bottom of every record, and figures that always reconcile. For someone whose day job is payments infrastructure this is the one direction where the visual language and the subject are the same thing.

The banding isn't a striped-table style choice — it's the stock. It runs behind everything on the page, which is what makes it a material rather than a component.

Palette
Two-tone stock: #f8f9f5 base with #eaf0e4 bands. Olive deepens slightly to #4d6129. Thread red #b0342a rules the top and bottom of each record.
Visual layer
The banded stock itself, plus reconciled figures — running totals, column sums, a footing line. Numbers are tabular and they add up, which is checkable.
Why it's livable
Banding is a background property, so every new page type inherits it for free. It's also the most durable: accounting stock has looked like this for a century and shows no sign of trending.
Risk
Green banded paper reads "spreadsheet" to some people before it reads "ledger." And it's the closest of the five to Round 1's catalog idea, so it may feel like ground you've already rejected.
